New Keywords
Sharpen

Put a +1{p} counter on the object. Remove all +1{p} counters from that object at the end of turn.

Sharpen is a new effect keyword available on some cards in Compendium of Rathe.
Sharpen is a discrete effect and a delayed-triggered effect. When you are instructed to sharpen, you will put a +1{p} counter on the targeted object or object specified after the sharpen keyword. At the end of the same turn you sharpened that object, sharpen’s delayed triggered effect triggers. You will remove all +1{p} counters on the sharpened object when that triggered effect resolves
When resolving Sharpen’s triggered effect, you will remove not only the +1{p} counter put on the object by the Sharpen effect, but all other +1{p} counters on the object are removed as well. All other counters remain on that object.
Non-sharpen effects that put +1{p} on an object do not count for the purposes of determining if an object was sharpened.
Resolving a sharpened effect on an object results in that object having been sharpened for the purposes of cards and effects.
When resolving a sharpen effect, if you are unable to put a +1{p} counter on the object, it will still count as having been sharpened. (This is similar to intimidating an opponent with an empty hand).
An object can have +1{p} counters and -1{p} counters at the same time. Adding +1{p} counters to an object that already has -1{p} counters does not cancel the existing token out.

Rip Off the Top

Brute Action

(YEL) 1{r} 2{d}

Draw a card, then pitch a random card from your hand. If it has 6 or more {p}, your next attack this turn gets +3{p}.

Go again

When resolving Rip Off the Top, you will draw a card first, then you will pitch a card randomly from your hand. If you successfully pitch a card this way, you will gain resource points equal to the pitched card’s value and if that pitched card has 6 or more {p}, your next attack this turn (e.g. weapon attack, ally attack, attack action card, etc.) will get +3{p}. 
When you pitch cards from resolving Rip Off the Top, it can still trigger effects due to pitching and you still gain the resources from pitching (e.g. you would gain 3 resources if it was a blue card, etc).
When resolving Rip Off the Top, there is no priority between any of the effects generated by the card (e.g. drawing a card, pitching a random card)
If you cannot draw a card, you will still randomly pitch a card.
If a card that is being pitched is in a private state, it is shown and made public first before it is moved to the pitch zone. If you are under an effect that says you can’t pitch a specific card (e.g. Chains of Eminence) you will not be able to pitch that card and it will remain in your hand. In this scenario, if you could not pitch the card and it has 6 or more {p}, you will not generate the if-conditional continuous effect and it will not apply to your next attack this turn.

(RED/YEL/BLU) 2{r} 6/5/4{p} 3{d}

Aggressive Pounce’s ability generates a conditional continuous effect that is always checking. If you haven’t intimidated an opponent yet this turn you when you attack with this and then you do, this will get go again.

You don’t retroactively get an action point from go again if you’ve passed the resolution step and you haven’t intimidated an opponent, and then you do later in the turn (e.g. after you’ve played another attack, closed the combat chain etc.)

Seismic Shift

Guardian Action

(RED) 0{r} 3{d}

As an additional cost to play this, {t} X Seismic Surge tokens you control.

Destroy X target aura tokens.

Go again

The additional cost value of tapping X Seismic Surge tokens is determined at the time the card is declared to be played. This is so that X targets can be declared for Seismic Shift’s second ability.
You must have a target to play Seismic Shift for any X>0 when paying its additional cost. E.g. You cannot play Seismic Shift tap 4 Seismic Surge tokens for its additional cost, if there are not at least 4 aura token targets.
You cannot target non-token auras with Seismic Shift.
As Seismic Surge tokens have the aura sub-type, you can tap to pay for the additional cost and also target them as well.
You can still play Seismic Shift if you declare X=0. Doing so means that you will not tap any Seismic Surge tokens you control when paying the additional cost. Additionally, when Seismic Shift resolves, you will not destroy any aura tokens. You will gain an action point from resolving go again.
If all targeted aura tokens are destroyed before Seismic Shift resolves, you will still gain an action point from go again.

Distant Rumbling

Guardian Action – Aura

(RED/YEL/BLU) 0{r} 3{d}

When this enters the arena, draw a card, then put a card from your hand into your deck fifth from the top.

At the start of your turn, destroy this, then create 3/2/a Seismic Surge tokens.

Distant Rumbling’s first ability triggers when it enters the arena (e.g. resolves on the stack or when it is put into the arena from another card’s effect). When this triggered effect resolves, you will first draw a card, then you will put a card from your hand into your deck fifth from the top. 
If you cannot draw a card, you will still put a card into your deck fifth from the top.
Fifth from the top means that there are exactly four cards above the card in the deck. If there are fewer than four cards in your deck, put the card on the bottom of your deck.
When resolving Distant Rumbling’s first triggered effect, there is no priority between any of the effects generated (e.g. drawing a card, putting a card into your deck).  
Distant Rumbling’s second ability triggers at the beginning of your start phase. Neither player receives priority during the start phase of a turn. When the triggered effect resolves, you will destroy this aura then immediately create the prescribed number of Seismic Surge tokens.
The Seismic Surge’s created by the second ability will trigger in the same turn they are created because Seismic surges trigger at the start of the action phase.

Become the Cup

Ninja Action – Attack

(RED/YEL/BLU) 0{r} 3/2/1{p} 2{d}

As you play this, choose a color. This gets the chosen color.

Go again

Become the Cup’s first ability is a static ability that becomes functional while playing it. It is not a triggered ability. While announcing that you are playing Become the Cup, you will choose a color and it will gain that color, in addition to its existing colors. Then as normal, you will apply effects to the card that matches the effects’ descriptions. For example, if you resolve Prismatic Leyline then play Become the Cup (red) and choose blue, that card will be both red and blue for the purpose of applying Prismatic Leyline’s continuous effects.
Become the Cup’s ability is not functional while defending.
If you do not play Become the Cup, you do not get to choose a color and it will not subsequently gain the color.
If an effect prevents you from playing a card of a certain color, you wouldn’t be able to play Become the Cup and choose that particular color as you play it.
If you are under an effect that causes cards you own to lose colors (e.g. Blanch) and you play Become the Cup, you will still be able to choose a color as you play it and Become the Cup will get that color.

Effects that simply let you look at the top of your deck (e.g. Dash I/O) will not be replaced by Topsy Turvy’s effect. Looking is different from putting a card on top of a deck: the two effects are distinct from each other. 
If an effect instructs you to look at the top card of your deck and then gives you an option to put it on the bottom (e.g. Cut to the Chase, Right Behind You, Hornet’s Sting), the card will remain on the top of the deck if you choose to not put it on the bottom. Looking is different from putting a card on top of a deck: the two effects are distinct from each other. 
If an effect tells you to look at the top card of your deck, then has subsequent conditional effects that can result in the card being placed either on the top or bottom of the deck (e.g. Pack Call, Firewall), that card will be put on the bottom of your deck under Topsy Turvy’s replacement effect. Looking is different from putting a card on top of a deck: the two effects are distinct from each other. If you were to normally meet the condition to put the card on top of your deck after looking at it, you would put it on the bottom instead.
If you were instructed to Opt 1 while under the effect of Topsy Turvy, you will look at the card; however, the card you look at will be put on the bottom of your deck as you will not be able to put it on the top as part of the Opt effect.
If an effect instructs you to put a card X cards from the top of your deck (e.g. Evo Steel Soul Controller, Submerge), Topsy Turvy’s effect will not modify it. The card will still go in the prescribed position.
If an additional cost requires you to put a card on the top of your deck (e.g. Seek Horizon), you will put the card on the bottom of your deck instead to fulfill that cost. Doing so will meet the condition of paying the additional cost for the purposes of cards and effects.
If an alternative cost allows you to put a card on the top of your deck instead of paying that card’s {r} cost (e.g. Moon Wish, Sun Kiss), you can still use that card’s alternative cost; however, you will instead put it on the bottom of your deck to fulfill that cost. Doing so will meet the condition of paying the additional cost for the purposes of cards and effects.
